# M05 · CRITICAL IMPACT · Eyes up

In-room · The threshold (phase 2) · within 10 sec of the door · Counter, from the door · threshold moment (thin-slice)

Dominant inputs: **non-verbal** and **brand**; BE present (thin-slicing, affect); concept sets the geometry (a counter, a door, no host stand).

## CURRENT STATE

She has stepped in from Rakovski (M3). The room is reading itself to her in the same second (M4: scent, low music, the lit display as the centre, the jars, the counter at level eyes). She is near her before-state: busy, a little drained, a day nobody marked. "This is real" moving to "They saw me." Next: the question (M7) when it is her turn, or the question overheard (M6) if there is a queue, with her own acknowledgement already given.

## DO

1. Whoever is nearest the door side of the counter looks up within 10 seconds of the door opening; at once if free. Mid-serve: a half-second away from the guest in hand and back. Eyes meet, a quick genuine smile, no word yet.
2. Eyes before words. The acknowledgement is the look and the smile. For a known guest, the locked line follows. For a first-timer there is no greeting formula: the first sentence she hears is the question (M7).
3. The look takes in one thing about her (a new haircut, a tired face, someone who came alone; v3 §3.2). It is used later (M10, M22), never announced at the door.
4. Posture: upright and relaxed, weight even, hands visible; a quarter-turn of the body to the door for the look; no leaning on the counter, no folded arms.
5. Queue: the newcomer gets the look within 10 seconds even with two waiting; then back to the guest in hand without hurrying them. The look says "seen"; it does not say "wait".
6. Two on the counter: the one not serving looks up; both serving, the nearer does.
7. The two steps from the door to the counter are silent. Nothing is called across the room; the room does the talking (S3-02 transposed to a counter).
8. The room's work in the same seconds (M4), checked before open: scent of dairy, vanilla and espresso and nothing else (cleaning finished and aired before open, v3 §7.7 Scent); music low enough to talk over, Italian acoustic by day; the display lit as the visual centre, tiramisu shown with the same care; the jars visible at every station; the counter at level eyes; the door closing softly; 20–22 °C.

## SAY

Known guest, **LOCKED**, name first where known:

> EN: "Good to see you again."
> BG: „Радваме се да те видим пак."

First-timer: no locked line. A plain hello at most; nothing added before the question (M7).

Registers:

| Register | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Regular | Name + the locked line |
| First-timer | The look and the smile; the question (M7) is the greeting |
| Group | The look to each face; the locked line once if one of them is known |
| Mid-serve, newcomer at the door | The look only; the line waits for conversational distance |
| Long wait (past the design's five minutes) | "Thanks for waiting." then the question. A fact, not an apology. |

Filter test (*Is this processing the guest?*): to her, no hurry, nothing sold. All **pass**. Register ти / warm "you".

## DON'T

- Don't greet the display with your head down.
- Don't "Welcome", "Welcome to Atelier del Gelato", "How can I help you?", "Next!".
- Don't call a regular's name across the room; the name is for conversational distance.
- Don't make the newcomer wait for the look until the current order is done; ten seconds is the ceiling.
- Don't perform the look (a big wave, a loud hello, the stock smile).
- Don't say what you noticed ("you look tired", "new haircut?").
- Don't hold the look until it becomes a demand to order now.
- Don't "sorry for the wait" for a normal wait; the brand's apology is the full ritual, unhurried.

## NON-VERBAL

- **Eyes** (NV C.2): meet hers for about a second with a quick genuine smile (Bible §9 welcome row: warmth, intensity 3, gaze at the guest); release back to the guest in hand, or hold softly until she reaches the counter if free. Between guests the face rests warm, never blank.
- **Posture** (NV C.1): relaxed-upright, hands visible and at rest or at the task; shoulders open to the door side.
- **Orientation** (NV C.4): a quarter-turn of the body to the door for the look; back to the guest in hand after.
- **Approach and distance** (NV A.1, A.2): head-on across the counter is the brand's geometry (a Peer meets you face to face); the counter width is the distance; no leaning across to shorten it.
- **Tone and volume** (NV B.1, B.3): if a word is said, calm and light, a notch warmer than neutral, for her; never a raised hello for the room.
- **Timing** (NV E.3) and **the wait** (NV E.2): within 10 seconds; the look is what turns a queue into a wait rather than a limbo.
- **Environment** (NV F.1–F.3): warm directed light, the sound register, the scent; DO 8.
- **Face**: open; the smile reaches the eyes; never the stock smile (v3 §6.4 refuses it in photographs; the counter refuses it in person).

## BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS

- **Thin-slicing** (BE 17; NV 3.2.3): the judgement of the place is complete before the first word, from cues she does not register: the face that looked up, the scent, the sound, the light, the door. In a counter format the team member's face is the single largest cue; this page is that cue.
- **Affect heuristic** (BE 5): her first emotional read colours the taste, the price and the farewell. Genuine warmth, not performed; the library's failure mode is the hired smile.
- **Cognitive ease** (BE 1): nothing to decide. The room is legible (display centre, jars, counter) and nothing she can see is announced (S3-03).
- **Social proof** (BE 11): other guests at the counter being asked the question (M6) tell her what this place is before she is asked.
- **Decision here**: none. The design removes "am I welcome, where do I go".

## RECOVERY

| If … | → |
| --- | --- |
| The look is late (back turned, busy) | As soon as noticed: eyes and smile; at her turn, the question first. No "sorry for the wait" for a normal wait; "Thanks for waiting." only past five minutes. |
| She hovers at the door, unsure | The look and a small nod toward the counter. No "come in, come in". |
| She is on the phone | The look and smile, no words. The question waits for the phone. If she stays on the phone at the counter, serve what she points to; the Note still goes; the question is asked once she is off, or not at all. The one case the question is skipped; logged at pre-shift. |
| A regular is not recognised by a new team member | The locked line is not used unless sure; the question stands. Names are a Stage 4 drill. |
| She arrives upset or in a hurry | Same look, same timing; the question still asked; her mood is not read aloud. |
| The room is wrong (cleaning smell, music loud, display unlit, jar hidden) | Fixed before the next guest; the look still comes first. Pre-shift checklist. R-ROOM. |
| Three or more waiting, one on the counter | The look within 10 seconds to each newcomer holds; the tasting (M11) is what gives. Named degradation at peak. R-QUEUE. |
