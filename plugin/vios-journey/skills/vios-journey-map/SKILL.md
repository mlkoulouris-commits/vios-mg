# Vios Journey Map

Stage 2. The map translates the brand into the moments where it has to act, what it has to do there, and which moments carry disproportionate weight in memory. It is a short strategic instrument (one or two pages in the manuscript, three or four in the house form), not an emotion diagram and not a touchpoint inventory. Hard lines: `vios-core:vios-guardrails` 10 (the founder signs the map), 11.

## Read first

- `../../references/stage-2-journey-map.md`, the chapter verbatim: six moves, the five-band template, patterns S2-01 to S2-05, the harvest. This skill applies it; it does not restate it.
- `[client]/brand-book.md` and `brand-book-vN.md`: the six intake elements of § 2.2.2.1, read in this order and held throughout: audience persona (all layers), Brand Role and Connection statement, brand archetypal mix, the eight Personality Expression dimensions, differentiator and positioning, core messages and story framework. No brand book, or a book without a Role and Connection statement: stop, `vios-brand` first. A map built on a brand the practice has not internalised maps to nothing.
- `[client]/fact-sheet.md`: the operation as it is (hours, service model, reservation route, the room).
- `../../references/section-3-3-three-registers.md`: the map is written in the operational register and points forward to floor scripts; it never contains them.

## House form (from the ONE Gallery map, the practice's worked deliverable)

- **Markers**: ★ signature (the one moment where the differentiator becomes visible), ◉ peak, ⊐ end (may be two moments, end-1 and end-2, designed as one), ○ silent. All four are named on the cover legend with the count of HIGH, MEDIUM and LOW moments.
- **One table per phase**, columns: code · moment · weight · brand intention · role-in-moment · dominant dimension · customer target state · marker. Not five separate bands; the bands are the columns. Codes are two-digit from the start (M01 … M31) so the design pages match. Silent rows carry code, name, ○ in weight and marker, and nothing else; the reason lives in the Reasoning Note. LOW rows keep the intention to twelve words or fewer; the full text lives in the inventory. A map of 25–35 moments renders in three or four pages with these rules; the manuscript's one or two is the target, not a rule.
- **Customer target state** is the guest's inner sentence, in quotes: "This is for me." "I know where to sit." "I'll come back."
- **Arc table**: phase · name · duration as a range ("~30 sec", "2–10 min", "days–weeks") · what the customer is doing. Then one line naming the peak, the end and the signature with their phases.
- **Summary section, "what passes to Stage 3"**: the HIGH table with "why HIGH" per moment; the silent table with "why silent"; the five-question coherence test as a table with YES/NO and the evidence.
- **Realignment**: when the brand book moves edition, the map is re-read against it, changed cells carry a `[vX.Y]` marker, and a cover note states what changed and what did not (weights, signature, peak, end, silences usually hold; what changes is outside the room).
- **Languages**: the repo holds the EN files (`CLAUDE.md`: English in the repo). The client-facing editions in the team's language (BG for Bulgarian teams) are redrafts, not translations, delivered as Claude Docs or Drive files and linked from `[client]/journey/README.md`; the account manager asks for them, the skill does not produce them unasked.
- **Claude Doc**: produced when the account manager asks for the deliverable edition, not as part of writing the files.

## Move 0 — Intake summary

One page, `[client]/journey/intake-stage-2.md`: the six elements in the practice's words, dated, with the brand book edition. It opens the Stage 2 file and is not part of the Reasoning Note. If any element is thin, say so here; the map inherits the gap.

## Move 1 — Establish the arc

Four to eight phases, named for this brand's customer, not the category (pattern S2-01). A phase whose customer has no persona on file (a B2B line, partner tables, event hosts) is mapped thin and MEDIUM-only with its target states marked "to confirm", and the Reasoning Note says a persona is needed before it can carry HIGH moments; or it is left out and named as a separate map to come. Say which. The arc starts before arrival (decision, booking) and ends after departure (post-visit) when the brand owns those phases. Mark each phase's share of the experience. Test: do the phases produce different moments, or only different names?

## Move 2 — Identify the moments

Walk each phase and ask three questions: what does the customer do, what does the brand do, what is at stake if either does it badly. Every answer that is not "nothing" is a moment. Capture operational moments and brand moments, all of them, in sequence, with the customer's action and the brand's action, coded M01, M02 … (two-digit, as the house form). Twenty to fifty is normal. Routine moments (coat, water, bathroom) are mapped, not skipped (pattern S2-04).

## Move 3 — Assign weight

HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW for every moment, from four sources, named per moment:

1. **Peak-end and duration neglect** (`vios-core:vios-be-lens`, Two Selves): name the peak and the end; both HIGH by default.
2. **Moments of decision**: order, drink, dessert, stay, return; usually HIGH.
3. **The brand's own logic**: where the differentiator becomes visible, where the Role is enacted (a Defender's threshold, a bar-led brand's every bar moment).
4. **The threshold thin-slice**: the first seconds inside are HIGH in every hospitality journey, whatever their length.

Restraint rule (pattern S2-02): three to seven HIGH out of the inventory. More than that and there is no hierarchy; renegotiate. Mark the **signature** ★ separately from the peak: the signature is the brand's identifying ritual (the differentiator made visible), the peak is the moment of highest memory weight; they are usually different moments. State the distribution as counts and shares of **all** moments, with silent moments as their own row. Do not aim at any earlier client's split; the numbers come from this brand's moments.

## Move 4 — Place the brand at each moment

Four fields per moment, in the operational register:

| Field | Draws on | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brand intention | Connection statement, core messages, the moment's place in the story framework | one sentence: what the guest should feel, understand or experience; name the **brand pillar** it serves (welcome, discovery, differentiator, quality …) |
| Role-in-moment | Brand Role | one phrase: how a Guide / Defender / Peer / Leader / Inspiration shows up here |
| Dominant expression dimension | the eight dimensions | one or two: appearance, dress, accessories, language, tone, volume, attitudes, beliefs |
| Customer target state | persona before-state → after-state | the guest's inner sentence in quotes, locating this moment on that arc |

HIGH moments get fuller text; LOW moments one line. Every placement derives from the brand book; a placement the book does not support is rewritten or the book is questioned.

## Move 5 — Name the silent moments

Where the brand deliberately does nothing: between two HIGH moments (pattern S2-03), during private activity (eating, talking, reading the menu), in transitions (the walk, the wait between courses). Silent is not LOW: LOW still acts a little, silent acts not at all. Mark each with its reason; Stage 3 receives them as instructions.

## Move 6 — Close the map

Five questions, each yes or no with the fix: arc specific to this customer · all moments named · real hierarchy in the weights · every placement from the brand book · silences read as choices, not gaps. Then write the **Reasoning Note** (pattern S2-05), in this order: purpose · why these phases (each departure from the category defended) · how the moments were identified and the density per phase · why each HIGH is HIGH · **close calls** (what was considered HIGH and is not, and why) · the signature explained · the peak explained · the end explained (when it is two moments, as one design) · each silence explained · a **brand-book coherence table** (moment · brand-book element · how it expresses) · close. Four to eight pages, read once by the founder, then archived.

## Output

1. `[client]/journey/journey-map.md` in the house form above: cover (venue, descriptor, stage, date, edition note), how to read, weight tiers with counts, markers, the arc table, one table per phase, the summary that passes to Stage 3. Three or four pages when rendered under the house-form rules above. The Claude Doc edition (landscape, readable at a glance by a founder or a designer) only when the account manager asks for it. The inventory of band 2 (customer action, brand action per moment) stays in `[client]/archive/journey-inventory.md`.
2. `[client]/journey/reasoning-note.md`.
3. `[client]/journey/handoff-to-design.md` (§ 2.2.6): constraints grouped by moment cluster, each as constraint · detail and source (the brand-book section or the operational fact, e.g. a named host's hours); the critical dependencies Stage 3 must flag; the convergent inputs per HIGH moment; where the design has latitude.
4. **Bridge to marketing**: update `[client]/brand-book.md` › Journey so the canonical touchpoints (`vios-core:vios-guardrails` › Journey) point at their moment codes, the peak and the signature are marked, the guest state per touchpoint is copied from the placement table, and the owner column is derived from the phase (pre-arrival and post-visit phases are marketing, in-venue phases are floor, unless the fact sheet says otherwise). `vios-content-calendar`, `vios-persona-voice` and `vios-persona-builder` read that table; the full map stays in `journey/`.
5. Brief revisions, if the map constrains the room: architect or interior-designer brief revision, versioned and dated (§ 2.2.6).
6. Harvest note (§ 2.2.7): arc patterns, moment patterns, template refinements, peak-end placements, in `[client]/archive/harvest-stage-2.md`.

End with: phase count, moment count, the HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW split, the signature, the peak and the end named, the silences, and the next step (`vios-journey-design`). The founder signs the Reasoning Note before design starts.

## Example (brewery, band 4 for one HIGH moment; placeholders)

**M14 First pour explained** · phase: making · HIGH ★ · intention (pillar: made together): what you are drinking was made here, together with what you will eat, on purpose · role-in-moment: Guide alongside, one sentence then step back · dominant dimensions: language and vocabulary, attitudes · target state: "Someone thought about this." Silent moment ○ follows: the first sip, no staff presence for two minutes.
