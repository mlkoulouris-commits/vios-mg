## CURRENT STATE

She is buying for someone else: a celebration box or a sharing tiramisu. She was asked the question (M09) and answered for the other person ("my sister's birthday"). She wants to get it right, and is spending more than usual.

## DO

1. **Counter**: the question first (M09), then the box line.
2. Choose together: offer the collection as five names, not twelve decisions; suggest choosing by rows (the classics, then the fruit), her call each time.
3. Close the box in her sight. One card from the jar into her hand (M18). Whether a card also goes in the box: **CONFIRM** (open-facts 46).
4. If the box is ordered ahead: say the day it is ready, once; take only the contact needed for the order (guardrails 12).
5. More time, same pace: never hurry because the line has grown; a colleague takes the line (R-QUEUE).

## DON'T

- No upsell of size ("the box of twelve is better value").
- No "everyone loves the pistachio" (social proof refused).
- No price pushed; the price once (M15).
- No promise of a day the Lab has not confirmed.

## BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS

BE 8 choice architecture: five names in two rows, chosen together, instead of twelve free picks. BE 14 decision fatigue: choosing by rows keeps the load low. BE 13 mental accounting: a gift budget is spent differently; the brand answers with time, not with upgrades.

## RECOVERY

If the box cannot be made today → say when it can (**CONFIRM**, 46), no apology speech (**R-SOLDOUT**). If she is unhappy with what is available → **R-UPSET**.
