## How to read

One table per phase. Columns: **code · moment · weight · brand intention (with the pillar it serves) · role-in-moment · dominant dimension · customer target state · marker**. The target state is the guest's inner sentence, in quotes. Silent moments carry no fields: the brand does nothing there, on purpose. The customer is **Elena** (Brand Book v3 §2.1): before-state *busy, a little drained, small wins passing unnoticed*; after-state *calm, seen, lighter, reconnected*. Phase 8 belongs to a second customer, the partner venue owner, whose persona is not yet on file.

## The arc

| Phase | Name | Duration | What the customer is doing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Before: the blur | days–weeks online · ~2 min on the street | Carrying a day nobody marked; seeing a real guest's note online; walking home along Rakovski and deciding to stop |
| 2 | The threshold | ~10–30 sec · plus 0–5 min if there is a queue | Stepping in, being seen, hearing the question (and hearing it asked of the person ahead) |
| 3 | Choosing together | 1–4 min | Answering or not, tasting, choosing a flavour for her moment |
| 4 | Marking it | 1–2 min | Watching it scooped, receiving the note, paying |
| 5 | The ten minutes | 5–15 min · or the walk home | Reading the card, the first taste, sitting with it |
| 6 | Leaving | ~30 sec | Being sent off by name |
| 7 | The card on the desk | days–weeks | Keeping the card, sharing it, coming back |
| 8 | The partner table (B2B) | parallel arc · delivery cadence to confirm | A venue owner choosing the Lab; a guest the brand never meets reading a story card |

Peak: **M15** (phase 4). End: **M22 + M23** (phase 6). Signature: **M7** (phase 2). The signature sits before the peak, by design: the question opens the ritual, the note closes it, and the two are the same promise seen twice.

## What passes to Stage 3

### The HIGH moments

| Code | Moment | Why HIGH |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M5 | Eyes up | The threshold thin-slice (Part III §3.2.3): she judges the place in the first seconds, and in a walk-in atelier the person at the counter is the place. Brand logic: "Every guest in our ateliers is greeted personally" is a standing commitment, and "Stay human" is the value the brand says it will pay for. |
| M7 | The question ★ | The differentiator made visible, every order, every atelier. Without it the ritual has no opening and the note is a loose card. |
| M11 | Tasting two together | Moment of decision (what to order), and the place where "no rushing" either holds or breaks; the category's weakest point (people, speed) is answered here. |
| M15 | The note from the jar ◉ | Peak-end: the surprise the remembering self keeps and carries home (story block 6: "It feels as if it was written for her"). Brand logic: the Atelier Note is the signature asset (v3 §5.4). |
| M22 | Farewell ⊐ end-1 | Peak-end: the end is remembered as much as the peak; the farewell line is the brand's belief said as a goodbye. |
| M23 | Attention held to the door ⊐ end-2 | The end does not finish at the line; it finishes when she is out of sight. A back turned to the next order undoes M22 in one second. Designed with M22 as one moment. |

### The silent moments

| Code | Moment | Why silent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M8 | The beat after the question | The question is a gift only if the answer is not demanded. The brand asks, then holds the silence; whatever comes (a win, a shrug, nothing) is received at M9. Filling the beat turns the question into a script. |
| M18 | Reading the card | Pattern S2-03: the silence after the peak. The card does its work privately; a team member watching her read it, or asking "did you like it", collapses the surprise into a performance. |
| M19 | The first taste | Private activity. No "how is it?", no presence; the brand's answer to "Taste" is already in the cup. |
