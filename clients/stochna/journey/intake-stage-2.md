## 1. Audience persona (all layers)

**The Social Seekers** (book § 2.1). 28–42, Sofia urban core (Lozenets, Centre, Oborishte), creative and knowledge workers, medium to high disposable income, social life identity-defining, 70% without children. Identity belief: "Where I spend my time with friends says something about who I am."

How they relate to hospitality: distrust large menus, feel relief when options are curated; comfortable staying 90–120 minutes; order in rounds; care where things are made and distrust resellers; interested in beer but not obsessive, appreciate pairing suggestions. Core pain: "Most places are either good for beer OR good for food OR good for hanging out. I want all three." Core challenge: "Finding a place that's genuinely worth the trip." Core belief: "If a place makes everything on site, you can taste the difference."

Archetypal profile: Everyman primary (belonging, equality, ease), Outlaw latent (admires calm refusal of the mainstream).

Emotional transformation, the three layers every target state in the map is located on:

| Layer | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Psychological | Scattered · socially cautious · deciding where to go | Grounded · open and easy · glad they came |
| Emotional | "Will this be worth the trip?" · "Another bar, another night" · "What should we order?" | "This is the real thing" · "That was a great evening" · "Everything here is good" |
| Identity | "Is there a place that gets it?" · "I like this" | "This is my place" · "I'm bringing everyone here" |

Behaviours: stay 90–120 minutes, order in rounds, ask questions and listen, share food and beer, bring friends and come back with different friends, photograph moments not products. How they talk: "You have to try this place." Loyalty triggers: consistency, staff who remember how they think, pairings that keep getting better, friends always have a good time.

Anti-persona: the Convenience Maximizer (speed, Wi-Fi, choice as value; party-first, trend hunters, status seekers). Stance: polite, clear, consistent, does not bend.

Thin: the chapter asks for four persona layers; the book gives demographics, psychographics, hospitality attitudes, archetypes and a three-layer transformation. Read as complete for the dining-room guest. No persona exists for event hosts or group bookings (the Memento events module exists); that line is not mapped, see `reasoning-note.md`.

## 2. Brand Role and Connection statement

**Role: Guide.** Book § 5.4 block 5: Stochna does not position itself as the hero; it acts as a guide, experienced, principled, warm, whose role is to create conditions, design great combinations and make the social experience effortless, through design and behaviour, not explanation. Social role (§ 2.2): a social anchor, the place people bring friends to, a reliable social constant in the middle-time zone of the evening; not nightlife, not a special-occasion venue, not a content backdrop.

**Connection statement: not written in the Stage 1 form.** The nearest the book gives is the core belief (§ 1.1: beer and food, made together in the same place, create the conditions for genuine social connection) and the job-to-be-done (§ 2.2: "Where do I take friends for a great evening?"). The map uses these two sentences as the Connection statement and marks it to confirm (`open-facts.md` row 3). The skill says to stop without a Role and Connection statement; the Role is explicit and the Connection is derivable from two book sentences, so the map proceeds and the gap is named here and in `blind-test-notes.md`.

## 3. Brand archetypal mix

Outlaw 70%, the principled one: quiet refusal of beer as inventory, food as fuel, hospitality as transaction; sets its own rules calmly; no explanatory marketing. Everyman 30%: everyone an equal, warmth replaces performance, knowledge shared never used as status. Creator as operational engine: present in what is made, not in the voice. Boundaries: never Performer, Sage or Lover. Character: **the Quiet Refuser** (§ 4.2): does not stop what they are doing to greet you, nods, gestures at the counter, keeps stirring; within ten minutes you are tearing herbs alongside them.

## 5. Differentiator and positioning

Positioning (§ 3.2): Stochna helps people who want genuine social experiences around beer and food discover what happens when both are made together in one place, unlike breweries that optimise for selection, entertainment or production, by integrating brewing and cooking as one system, offering only proper choices and designing everything around the table.

Differentiator (§ 3.3): brewing and cooking as one integrated system, and the entire experience designed around the social connection this creates. Structural, not cosmetic; costs accepted: complexity, fewer items, coordination. Descriptor: Kitchen Brewery. Tagline: "Cook for beer. Brew for food." Competitors optimise for curation and atmosphere (Kanaal), scale and entertainment (White Stork), selection depth (Vitamin B), production (Sofia Electric). The map's signature must be the moment where "made together" is seen, not told.

## 6. Core messages and story framework

Seven core messages (three primary: made together · proper choices · worth the trip; four secondary: table is the centre · proper is better than more · place matters · human, not performed). Story in seven blocks, guest as hero, Stochna as guide, experienced not told; the transformation condensed into "You have to try this place." Five micro-message blocks in the book (arrival and first contact · ordering and choice · making and explanation · the evening unfolds · departure and memory) plus two online blocks added in `brand-book.md` (discovery to reminder · after-visit and review). The five in-venue blocks are already the spine of the in-venue phases; the map keeps their order and adds the moments inside them.
