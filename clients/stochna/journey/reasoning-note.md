Written in the operational register. It explains the map's decisions; it does not contain floor scripts (those are Stage 3). Where a line from the book is quoted as a candidate script, it is a candidate, not a locked line.

## 1. Purpose

The map translates the Stochna Brand Book V2 into the moments where the brand has to act during an evening, what it has to do there, and which six moments carry the weight of memory. It was built from the book, the fact sheet and the practice's method only. No service was shadowed and no reviews were read before weighing (Move 2b), so every weight is provisional and every HIGH is brand-led. The note says so at each HIGH. The first thing this map asks of the engagement is the observation that will confirm or move those weights.

## 2. Why these phases

Seven phases: Deciding · The trip · The threshold · The first round · The evening · Leaving · After. Three departures from the category arc (decision, arrival, seating, ordering, service, payment, departure, post-visit), each defended:

- **The trip is its own phase.** The category treats the journey to the door as nothing. The book makes the 2 km into Stochna Gara a filter, not a drawback (§ 1.5: "people who come here choose to come here"; message 3: "2km from center. Worth it. Everyone here came on purpose"). The phase produces a moment the category arc does not have (M06, the street and the door) and holds the question the whole evening answers: was it worth it. It is one moment long on purpose; the brand's action there is to leave the street as it is and make the door and the window do the work (§ 6.7 success metric).
- **Seating is folded into the threshold.** For a kitchen-scale room where the greeting is "sit anywhere" (§ 4.5) and the Quiet Refuser "nods, gestures at the counter and keeps stirring" (§ 4.2), seating is not a phase; it is the second half of the first minute. Making it a phase would invite a host stand and an escort the book refuses.
- **The first round is separated from the evening.** The book's blocks 2 (ordering and choice) and 3 (making and explanation) are decision and signature moments where the brand acts; block 4 (the evening unfolds) is a phase where the brand mostly withdraws. Keeping them in one "service" phase would hide that the brand's density drops sharply after the first plates. The split is the map's hierarchy made visible in time.
- **Payment and departure are one phase, Leaving.** The book treats them as one block (block 5: settling the bill, final exchange, walking out) and the end is designed as one across two moments.
- **After is a phase the brand owns.** The transformation is a sentence said to a friend ("You have to try this place") and a return with different friends. The brand acts there: it stays findable and the same, answers reviews by the detail named, announces the next brew as fact.

Two arcs are not mapped. **Weekend day service** (from 13:00, exact doors to confirm) is a different customer rhythm; the book describes an evening ("where people go when they want a proper evening out", § 2.2) and the fact sheet gives no day persona. It is a map to come if the owner confirms the day guest differs. **The events line** (Memento events module: enquire, join, offer) has no persona on file; it is not mapped and would be MEDIUM-only with target states to confirm until Stage 1 gives it a persona. Both are rows in `open-facts.md`.

## 3. How the moments were identified; density per phase

Each phase was walked with the three questions (what does the customer do, what does the brand do, what is at stake). The book's five micro-message blocks gave the spine of phases 3 to 6; the two online blocks in `brand-book.md` gave phases 1 and 7; the fact sheet gave the booking route and hours. Routine moments (water, coats, bathroom) are mapped under pattern S2-04. Five moments are conditional (M14, M21, M27, M28, and M36 as to confirm) and four are standing (M20, M22, M23, M26); both kinds are flagged so Stage 3 designs a trigger for the first and a standard for the second.

Density is highest in the first round, where the brand does most of its visible work in about fifteen minutes, and lowest per minute in the evening, where the brand withdraws for an hour or more. That is the shape the book asks for: "the room should do the talking" (§ 4.7).

## 4. Why each HIGH is HIGH

All six are **brand-led**: no observation stands behind them yet.

**M07 Crossing the threshold.** The threshold is HIGH in every hospitality journey (§ 2.2.2.4, the thin-slice). For Stochna it carries a second load: the guest has travelled 2 km on purpose and the first seconds answer "was it worth it" (block 1 tension). The book specifies what the first seconds contain: smell of food and beer, visible production, people already having a good time, relaxed pacing (block 1 non-verbal). The brand's act is to make sure the room is doing that work before anyone speaks; the nod is the Quiet Refuser's greeting (§ 4.2). Dominant dimensions: appearance, volume and sound, attitudes. Thin-slicing is a contested heuristic in the practice's evidence table; it is used here as a design heuristic, not as a measurement claim.

**M15 The order for the table.** A decision moment (§ 2.2.2.4) and the moment where message 2 is tested: "Is this proper menu a sign of care or a sign of limitation?" (block 2). The persona distrusts large menus and feels relief when options are curated (§ 2.1), so the stakes are high in both directions. The brand's act is one linking suggestion ("this goes well with that") and the offer to pour if unsure (§ 4.5, § 4.6), then withdrawal. The order is taken for the table (message 4: ordering for the table, shared plates, rounds). No upsell (block 2 non-verbal).

**M17 First pour and the bread ★.** The signature. See section 6.

**M24 The next round, unhurried ◉.** The peak. See section 7.

**M30 The bill, when asked ⊐ · M31 "See you next time" ⊐.** The end. See section 8.

## 5. Close calls

Considered HIGH and placed lower, with the reason:

- **M03 Weighing the trip (MEDIUM).** A decision moment (whether to come at all) and the home of message 3. Placed MEDIUM because the brand's action there is small and online (state the distance, do not apologise); the decision is made on a friend's word (M01) more than on anything the brand does, and the book says message 3 "lives in the experience itself, word-of-mouth". If the review read shows guests mentioning the trip unprompted, it moves up.
- **M08 The greeting (MEDIUM).** Block 1 covers greeting and threshold together. The book makes the greeting deliberately small (a nod; "First time? Good. Sit anywhere"), so the weight sits on the room (M07), not the line. Making both HIGH would put two HIGHs inside one minute and push Stage 3 toward a ceremonial welcome the book refuses.
- **M19 First plates land (MEDIUM).** The plates are where "food designed for the beer" is tasted, so it was a candidate signature. The pour-and-bread pair was chosen instead because it arrives first, at every table, in two objects that need one sentence; the plates follow and inherit the sentence. If observation shows the plates are where guests take the photo "of the combination" (block 3 success signal), the signature is re-read.
- **M25 Plates cleared, nothing pushed (MEDIUM).** Clearing is where urgency leaks in most rooms and the book names "clearing pressure" as a failure (block 4). Kept MEDIUM because it is a refusal to act rather than an act; the Stage 3 design page for M24 covers it as the same standard.
- **M36 Coming back (MEDIUM, to confirm).** The loyalty triggers (§ 2.1) make the return a candidate HIGH, but whether the floor can see a returning booking is unknown (`open-facts.md` row 5). It cannot carry HIGH until the operation can act on it.

## 6. The signature: M17 First pour and the bread ★

The differentiator is "we brew and cook as one integrated system" (§ 3.3), and the book names where message 1 lives: visible production, menu pairings, staff explaining how a dish was designed for a specific beer, the tagline itself (§ 5.2). Block 3 gives the sentence: "The spent grain in this bread came from this morning's brew." The signature is the moment those two things are on the table together: the first beer, brewed here, and the bread, baked here with that beer's spent grain, landing at once, with one plain sentence and then nothing. It is "Cook for beer. Brew for food." in two objects. It happens at every table, every evening, within fifteen minutes of sitting down, which is what a signature needs: repeatable, early, ownable, and impossible for the four competitors (Kanaal curates others' beer, White Stork does not brew on site, Vitamin B has no food, Sofia Electric serves its beer elsewhere). It depends on two facts to confirm: that bread with spent grain is baked daily and that the first beer and the bread can be timed together (`open-facts.md` row 6). If either fails, the signature moves to M19, the first plates.

Why not the threshold or the peak: the threshold shows the making (equipment, smell) but does not put the integration in the guest's hands; the peak is about the table, not the product. The signature and the peak are different moments, as they usually are.

## 7. The peak: M24 The next round, unhurried ◉

The book defines the transformation in block 4 and § 5.4: "People stay longer without planning to. They order another round because the conversation is good." The persona "orders in rounds, not efficiently" and is "comfortable staying 90–120 minutes". The peak is therefore not a plate and not a pour; it is the moment the table, already at full tilt, is offered the next beer tied to what is coming from the kitchen, when it is ready and not before, and stays without having decided to. It is a decision moment (stay or move on, block 4 tension) that the brand resolves by not pressing it. Dominant dimensions: tone (suggests without pushing), language (the book's own line: "there's a darker one that goes well with what's coming next, want to try it?"), volume and sound (the room still lets the table hear itself). Failure modes from the book: too-frequent check-ins, managed energy, implied urgency.

This is the practice's peak-end logic (evidence tier) applied with the book's own definition of success. The remembering self takes home "best evening out I've had in months" (§ 2.1), and the second round is where that sentence is formed. Observation may move the peak toward M19 (the plates) if guests talk about the food more than the evening; the book predicts they will talk about the evening.

## 8. The end: M30 and M31, designed as one

The end is two moments, the bill and the farewell, designed as one because the book treats them as one block (block 5) and because the failure mode of each undoes the other: a bill with a review request on it spoils the warmest farewell, and a perfect bill followed by a scripted "thank you for visiting" ends the evening on a transaction. The standard across both: nothing requested (no review, no loyalty, no rating), nothing rushed, and a farewell that is a prediction ("See you next time", block 5; "Bring friends. There's room", § 4.6). Guardrails 2 (never gate or reward reviews) and the book agree; the review is asked nowhere in the arc.

The end is followed by a silence (M32, the walk out) so that the last words stay the last words.

## 9. Each silence explained

- **M10 Coats and settling.** The room's first minute with the guest. The book's character "doesn't stop what they're doing to greet you"; a staff member hovering through the settle would replace the room's work with presence. Silent after the threshold HIGH, a variant of pattern S2-03.
- **M13 Reading the menu.** Private activity. A short menu needs no presentation; explaining why it is short is a lecture (block 2 failure mode; § 4.7 "if you have to explain it, you haven't built it yet").
- **M16 The wait for the pour.** Between two HIGH moments (M15 → M17), pattern S2-03. The table absorbs its own decision; nobody previews what is coming. The pour is visible at the bar, which is the only brand presence.
- **M18 First sip.** After the signature. Two minutes of no staff presence so the sentence lands and the table tastes and passes glasses (block 3 success signal: share a tasting with the table).
- **M22 Conversation takes over.** The standing silence of the whole evening phase: the table is the centre, time becomes elastic, no check-ins (block 4). The brand's only act in that phase, reading the table from a distance (M23), is LOW, not silent, because watching is an act; the silence is at the table.
- **M32 The walk out.** After the end. No second goodbye, no card, no flyer, so the farewell is the last thing said.

Silent is not LOW: M11 (water) and M23 (reading the table) act a little; the six silences act not at all. Stage 3 receives them as instructions.

## 11. Close

The map closes the five coherence tests on the book. It does not close on the operation: no service was shadowed and no review was read. Before the founder signs, the account manager schedules two shadowed services on different dayparts (one weeknight at doors, one late or weekend) and reads the last 50 Google reviews, tagging each remark to a moment code, into `observation.md`. This note is then updated with the weights the observation changed. The re-weighting date is forty-five days after the Stage 3 design goes live, owned by the account manager.

Three decisions in this note were taken without the book's explicit support and are marked for the founder: the Connection statement derived from § 1.1 and § 2.2; the signature as the pour-and-bread pair (depends on daily spent-grain bread); the end split in two. Everything else traces to a book section in the table above.
