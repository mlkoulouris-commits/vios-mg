## Realignment v1.1

**What held:** the arc (7 phases), all 36 moments, every weight, the signature M17 ★, the peak M24 ◉, the end M30 + M31 ⊐, the six silences. Stage 1 added no new brand fact; it confirmed the book.

**What changed** (outside the room, as expected):
1. **AI ambassador** (`persona-bible.md` § 8). It speaks at M01, M02, M33, M35; it is silent at M04, M05 (booking and reminder go in brand voice, unsigned) and M34 (reviews, a human signs). It never appears in phases 2–6: every in-venue moment stays human.
2. **Risk at M02.** The target state "It's someone's place, not a concept" is the one an AI host can break: a labelled AI on the profile may read as concept. Not re-weighted; the persona pilot (Bible § 15) tests it, and a fail sends M02 back to people-only.
3. **M30 conflict named.** The Razberry review QR cards on file vs "nothing is requested" at the bill (`open-facts.md` row 23). The intention holds; the cards are the operational question.
4. **M17 claim.** The spent-grain sentence stays a candidate until open fact 6 confirms the bread.

## How to read

One table per phase. Columns: code · moment · weight · brand intention (with the pillar it serves) · role-in-moment (how the Guide shows up here) · dominant expression dimension · customer target state (the guest's inner sentence) · marker. Silent rows carry only code, name and ○; their reasons are in the Reasoning Note. LOW rows keep the intention to twelve words. The customer's and the brand's action per moment are in `archive/journey-inventory.md`. "Conditional" moments happen at some tables; "standing" moments run across the phase.

## The arc

| Phase | Name | Duration | Share of the visit | What the customer is doing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Deciding | days–hours | before the visit | Choosing where to take friends for an evening; weighing the trip; booking |
| 2 | The trip | 10–30 min | before the door | Travelling into an industrial zone on purpose; finding the door |
| 3 | The threshold | ~30 sec–3 min | ~2% | First seconds inside; greeting; finding the table; settling |
| 4 | The first round | 10–25 min | ~15% | Reading a short menu; ordering for the table; the first pour and plates; the making in view |
| 5 | The evening | 60–100 min | ~75% | Talking; the next round; staying without deciding to |
| 6 | Leaving | 5–10 min | ~6% | Asking for the bill; the farewell; the walk out |
| 7 | After | days–weeks | after the visit | Telling friends; the review; coming back with different friends |

Shares are of a 90–120 minute visit (book § 2.1). The peak is in phase 5, the end in phase 6, the signature in phase 4.

### The silent moments

| Code | Moment | Why silent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M10 | Coats and settling | Transition: the room's first minute with the guest; a staff member hovering would take it (pattern S2-03 variant: silence after the threshold) |
| M13 | Reading the menu | Private activity: the table reads and talks; a short menu needs no presentation (§ 4.7: the room should do the talking) |
| M16 | The wait for the pour | Transition between two HIGH moments (M15 → M17); the table absorbs its own decision (pattern S2-03) |
| M18 | First sip | After the signature: the table tastes and passes glasses; two minutes with no staff presence (pattern S2-03) |
| M22 | Conversation takes over | Private activity across the whole phase: "the table is the center"; no check-ins (block 4 failure mode: staff check in too frequently) |
| M32 | The walk out | After the end: a second goodbye, a card or a flyer would undo the farewell (block 5: the exit must not feel procedural) |
