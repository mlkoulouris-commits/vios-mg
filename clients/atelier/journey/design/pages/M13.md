# M13 · HIGH IMPACT · Two tasted together

**Draft — book edition v1.0 unsigned (open-facts 39); observation move not held.**

In-room · Phase 3 The question · ~20–40 sec · Both counters (Ring Mall tasting practice: open-facts 11, 55) · markers none · weight provisional

Dominant inputs: **brand** (message 3 The pause; Bible "want to taste two together?") · **non-verbal** (handling, silence) · **BE** (choice architecture). Concept consulted: tasting spoons and practice (open-facts 55).

## CURRENT STATE

She hesitates between flavours, or is a first-timer (M12). Tasting is usually granted reluctantly elsewhere. Here it is given.

## DO

1. **Counter**: offer, unasked, when she hesitates or is new.
2. Two spoons at once, handle toward her, one in each hand or side by side on the counter edge.
3. Step back half a step while she tastes; silence.
4. Accept her pick; if she wants a third taste, give it without comment.
5. Spoons into the bin at once, out of her way.

## SAY

| | EN | BG |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Offer | "Want to taste two together?" | **CONFIRM**: Bible §6 „Искаш ли да опиташ два заедно?" or §7 „Да опитаме два заедно?" (approver picks, open-facts 52) |

EN **LOCKED** (Bible §6, §7). BG locks when the approver chooses. Filter test: **pass**.

## DON'T

- No "you can only try one" or "just one taste, there's a queue".
- No pitch between the two spoons.
- No explanation of why two.
- No asking "which one did you like?" before she says.

## NON-VERBAL

Spoons handed handle-first (NV D.3); the half step back and silence while she tastes (NV A.1, B.4); face resting warm.

## BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS

BE 8 choice architecture: two together turns eight options into one comparison. BE 14 decision fatigue: a taste replaces deliberation.

## RECOVERY

If a line builds while she tastes → do not hurry her; **R-QUEUE**. If no spoons → say so plainly, offer a look at the case instead (**R-ROOM**).
