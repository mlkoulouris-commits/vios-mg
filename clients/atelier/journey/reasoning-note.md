## 1. Purpose

The map says where the brand has to act across Elena's visit, what it has to do there, and which moments her remembering self will keep. It is built for a walk-in gelato and tiramisu atelier with no bookings and a ten-minute visit, not for a restaurant: the arc is short in the room and long outside it. Stage 3 designs the six HIGH moments and the three silences from this map; nothing else in the map is a script.

## 2. Why these phases

Eight phases. Each departure from the category default is defended here.

| Phase | Why it exists for Elena, not for a gelateria in general |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Before: the blur | The story framework (v3 §4.1–4.3) places the call to action on the street: she is walking home with a win nobody marked. The category arc starts at "arrival"; Elena's starts at the unmarked win and the window on Rakovski. The brand owns this phase through the Instagram feed (real moments, the question) and the lit window; it does not own a booking, so there is none. |
| 2 The threshold | Kept, because the threshold is HIGH in every hospitality journey; redrawn, because in a counter atelier the threshold holds the signature too. The question is asked within the first thirty seconds, so the threshold phase ends with the ritual opened, not with a seat. The queue (M6) lives here because a walk-in guest's first minute is often spent watching the guest ahead. |
| 3 Choosing together | The category calls this "ordering". Here the choice is the second step of the ritual (ask · choose together · mark it) and the moment the Peer role is most visible: the team member chooses alongside her. |
| 4 Marking it | The category has "making" and "payment". Here the making is quiet by rule (no theatre; alla minute retired) and the note is the peak. Folding the note into "ordering" would bury the peak inside a transaction. |
| 5 The ten minutes | A restaurant has "the evening"; Elena has ten minutes, or the walk home. The phase is mostly silence (reading the card, the first taste) with the room doing background work. Named for its length because duration neglect means these ten minutes are remembered through their peak and end, not their length. |
| 6 Leaving | Thirty seconds, two moments, both HIGH. In a counter format the end is at risk of being swallowed by the next order; the phase exists so that it cannot be. |
| 7 The card on the desk | The category ends at "post-visit: review request". Elena's post-visit is an object she keeps (v3 §4.7), then sharing, then coming back. The brand's presence is the card, and the brand is silent as a voice except to answer. |
| 8 The partner table | The Vision puts the question at partner tables; the Lab's B2B is 53 % of its sales (diagnosis §2). It is a parallel arc with a different customer, mapped thin because that customer has no persona yet. Left off, the map would contradict the Vision; mapped fully, it would invent a customer. |

What was not done: a separate Ring Mall arc. The diagnosis flags the mall queue as the hard case for "Notice the moment" and Evo has not answered. The map treats Ring Mall as the same arc with M6 and M11 under pressure; if the answer is "the ritual is shortened at the mall", the mall becomes a second expression and gets its own phase-2 and phase-3 tables with `[v3.1]` markers.

## 4. Why each HIGH is HIGH

**M5 Eyes up.** Source: the threshold thin-slice. In a counter atelier there is no host stand, no walk, no table: the first seconds are a face behind a counter. The brand book makes this a standing commitment ("every guest greeted personally"), a value with a stated cost ("Stay human: no scripts, no kiosks") and a standard ("acknowledged within 10 seconds, eye contact and a smile before words"). The category's most common complaint is people, not product (v3 §2.2); this is where Atelier answers it first.

**M7 The question ★.** Source: the brand's own logic. The differentiator has one visible opening line, and it is this one. It is also a moment of decision in disguise: she decides whether to say what today meant. The question is HIGH because without it nothing that follows is a ritual; it is the signature because it is the identifying act that no competitor performs.

**M11 Tasting two together.** Source: moment of decision (what to order) and brand logic ("no rushing the decision", "Take your time"). The brand's slowness is the operational cost of the Lover archetype and the exact opposite of the category's "efficient, not personal". It is HIGH because it is where the promise of a pause is either kept or broken by the queue.

**M15 The note from the jar ◉.** Source: peak-end. The story framework's transformation turns on the card ("It feels as if it was written for her"), and the brand book names the Note the signature asset. The hand-over is the brand's last act before the silence in which the memory forms.

**M22 Farewell ⊐ end-1.** Source: peak-end. "Until the next celebration." is the brand's belief said as goodbye and the last words she hears. One line only: a second line turns a farewell into a push.

**M23 Attention held to the door ⊐ end-2.** Source: peak-end, non-verbal. The standard "attention held until the guest has left" (v3 §7.6) is a separate act from the line: the line can be perfect and the back can turn. In a counter format the next guest is already there, so the end is designed as two moments performed as one.

## 5. Close calls

| Considered HIGH | Decided | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M4 First look inside | MEDIUM | The room is read in the same second as the face, and the brand book says the ritual "needs no theatre, only people". The thin-slice weight goes to the person (M5). The room is the designer's and architect's brief, not Stage 3's script. |
| M9 The answer, or none | MEDIUM | The stake is real (pushing a guest who has nothing to celebrate breaks the Peer role), but the brand's act is small: receive, never push. It is designed inside M7's page as the "no answer" branch, not as its own HIGH. |
| M10 A flavour for the moment | MEDIUM | "Choose together" is the second step of the ritual, so it was a candidate. It is MEDIUM because its weight is carried by M11 (the tasting, where the decision lands); two HIGH moments a few seconds apart would have needed a silence between them that the counter cannot give. |
| M28 Coming back | MEDIUM | The decision to return is the one the remembering self makes, which argues HIGH. But the brand acts at M22–M23 and M15, not at M28; on the return visit M28 becomes M5 again ("Good to see you again"). Weighting it HIGH would design the same moment twice. |
| M31 The story card at the partner's table | MEDIUM | The question travelling is the Vision, which argues HIGH. The brand cannot act there; the partner does. HIGH would commit Stage 3 to designing a moment the brand does not perform. Revisit when a B2B persona and a partner agreement exist. |

## 6. The signature explained

M7, the question. The signature is the identifying ritual: what a guest would describe to a friend as "the place where they ask you what you're celebrating". It sits before the peak, which is unusual; the brand book's own three-step ritual puts the question first and the note last, so the map follows it. The signature carries the Peer role in one word: "we".

## 7. The peak explained

M15, the note from the jar. Three reasons. Peak-end: a surprise that leaves with the guest is the strongest memory a ten-minute visit can produce. Brand book: the Atelier Note is named the signature asset and the designer is told to build the system around it. Story: the transformation block is the card. The peak is placed on the hand-over, the brand's act, with the reading (M18) silent so the memory forms without a witness. Risk named for Stage 3: if the note is handed with the bill or dropped into the bag unspoken, the peak moves to the tasting (M11) and the card becomes packaging.

## 8. The end explained

M22 and M23 as one design. The end of a counter visit is thirty seconds in which the next guest is already waiting; the risk is a correct line with a turned back. End-1 is the line, by name where known; end-2 is attention held until she is out of the door. Together they are the Stay human value performed at the one place the remembering self weighs as heavily as the peak. For a to-go order the end is at the hand-over of the bag plus the same held attention; the duration changes, the two moments do not.

## 9. Each silence explained

**M8 The beat.** After the question, nothing. The persona's own rule is "asks before she tells" and the voice spec writes in "a pause before the question"; the map adds the pause after it. A beat lets a guest decline without feeling she failed a test. Failure mode: the team member answers for her ("Friday, right?") and the question becomes a greeting formula.

**M18 Reading the card.** The peak is absorbed in private (pattern S2-03). The brand's presence while she reads turns the surprise into a performance she must react to. Failure mode: "Which one did you get?"

**M19 The first taste.** Private activity. The brand's answer to the taste is in the cup; a question about it asks her to rate what she came to enjoy. Failure mode: the hospitality default "everything all right?"

## 11. Close

The map is defensible from the brand book at every cell it fills, and honest about the cells it leaves open: the mall queue, the B2B customer, the room facts, the prices and the age line. Its hierarchy is six HIGH moments that are the ritual's three steps (M7, M11, M15), the threshold (M5) and the end (M22–M23); everything else is MEDIUM or LOW so that those six can be designed to the last second. Signed, it goes to `vios-journey-design`.
