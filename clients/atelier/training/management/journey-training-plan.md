## 1. Five training principles

1. **Foundations before moments.** The three non-negotiables and the six continuities are learned once, in Session 1, so they are not re-taught at every moment.
2. **Say it, then walk it.** Every locked line is said aloud first; then said again at the real counter with the geometry: the case between, eyes first, the half step back.
3. **The retreat is the lesson.** As much time goes on what the team member does not do (the half step back, eyes off her card, no check-back) as on what she does. On a counter the retreat is half a step, not a walk away.
4. **Drill the counter-intuitive.** There is no welcome script; there is no bestseller; there is no "anything else?"; after the question, nothing. The instinct to help and to fill is trained out.
5. **Floor-ready is a checklist, not a feeling.** Sign-off is against `sign-off-checklist.md`, signed by both.

## 2. The foundations (Session 1 content)

### The three non-negotiables

Drawn by the practice from the handoff's "what the design must not do" (`journey/handoff-to-design.md` § E) and the peak cluster; the handoff names no three, so the approver confirms (open-facts 70):

1. **The question, whole, every order.** Never shortened, never skipped for a queue, a regular or closing time (M09).
2. **The card drawn blind and handed before the till.** Never chosen, never bagged, never with the receipt (M18, M19).
3. **The goodbye to her, not to the next guest.** Words to her face, eyes to the door, then the next guest (M25, M26).

### The six continuities (`choreography.md` § 1)

| Continuity | Floor line |
| --- | --- |
| One face for the ritual | "You asked. You hand the card. You say goodbye." |
| Eyes before words | "Look first. Then speak." |
| One line, a beat, then the hands | "Say it. Stop. Then move." |
| Where the team stands | "Behind the case. Face the door. Never both backs to it." |
| One voice | "We. Today. The question. The card. Never 'next'." |
| The end, then the next | "She leaves first. Then the next one." |

### The one safety rule

R-ESC: illness, an allergic reaction, injury, choking, a fall, legal, press, a complaint about a team member, discrimination, harassment → stop, 112 if it is safety, get the Lead, say nothing else. Taught in Session 1 because a trainee can meet it on the first shadow shift. Incident procedure and backup person: open-facts 58, 59.

### Session 5 — The silences and the three drills (60 min; last; never skipped)

**The four silences (20 min), each under a clock:**

| Silence | Standard | Clock |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M08 She reads the case | say nothing; half step back; wait for her eyes | stand-in reads for 30 s; any word is a fail |
| M10 The beat | nothing after the question | stand-in waits 3 s before answering; the trainee holds; past 3 s still nothing (choreography § 2) |
| M22 She reads the card | no comment, eyes off the card | 20 s |
| M23 The pause (RK) | no check-back, no clearing, body to the counter | a seated stand-in for **10 minutes** (inside the page's 5–15) |

**The three drills (40 min):**

1. **The retreat drill.** After the hello, after the spoons, after the card: half a step back and eyes to a quiet task. No glance back at the case, the spoons or her card. **The glance is the tell**; the trainer watches for it and calls it.
2. **The silent-moment drill.** A seated stand-in and a clock. The trainee works the counter for ten minutes and does not go to the table. It should feel uncomfortable before it feels normal. The beat is drilled the same way: ten questions in a row, each followed by silence until the stand-in speaks.
3. **The welcome-symmetry drill.** The hello (M06) and the goodbye (M25) back to back: same face, same plainness, same brevity. Then the counter composition: a leaver and a newcomer at once, two on and one alone, five times each.

Assessment: the four silences held under the clock; the three drills passed without the glance back.
