# Reasoning Note — Atelier del Gelato journey map

**Draft — book edition v1.0 unsigned (open-facts 39); observation move not held.**

Date 2026-11-01. Companion to `journey-map.md`; read once by Evo and signed before Stage 3 designs (open-facts 49), then archived. Written in the concept register. Every weight is provisional: no service has been shadowed and no review read (`observation.md`); the HIGH moments are brand-led, which this note says each time.

## 1. Purpose

The map says where Atelier del Gelato has to act across Elena's arc, what it has to do there, and which moments carry disproportionate weight in her memory of a visit. It is built for one persona (Elena, Creator 70 / Lover 30), one Role (Peer) and one differentiator (the question and the Note), at two counters and on a partner line. It is not an emotion diagram and not a touchpoint inventory; the inventory is in `archive/journey-inventory.md`.

## 2. Why these phases

A category gelato arc is decide → queue → order → pay → eat → leave. This map departs from it in five places, each defended:

- **The walk home (phase 1)** instead of "discovery". Elena's decision is made in seconds on the street or in the mall flow, not in a booking; the brand owns the online discovery (M01) because the baseline shows the audience voting for real people and the account not giving it. The phase holds both the online and the street moment because for a walk-in they are the same decision made twice.
- **The threshold (2)** kept as its own phase of ten seconds, with the weight on a face (M06) rather than the room (M05): the brand book's differentiator "needs no theatre, only people", and the persona's fear is being processed, not being under-lit. The Ring Mall queue is a threshold moment (M07), not an ordering one, because the thin-slice at the mall is formed in the queue.
- **The question (3)** as a phase of its own, separate from "marked" (4). The category has one ordering phase. Here the order has two halves with different actors' states: the asking and receiving (M09–M11), where she is exposed; and the making and marking (M17–M18), where she receives. Stage 3 needs them as separate pages because the geometry, the script and the recovery differ.
- **The pause (5)**: the purpose's own word; the category calls it "consumption" and designs nothing. Here it is designed as silence, which is a design.
- **The card on the desk (7)** and **the partner table (8)** are phases the brand owns beyond the door: the Note is built to outlive the visit (message 5), and the Vision names partner tables as where the brand is found. Phase 8 is mapped thin and MEDIUM-only because its customer, the venue owner, has no persona on file (open-facts 19); a persona is needed before it can carry a HIGH moment. It is kept on the map rather than left for a separate map because leaving it off would say the map does not believe the Vision.

The test (pattern S2-01): the phases produce different moments, not only different names; "the question" produces the beat of silence and the acceptance of "nothing", which no category arc has.

## 3. How the moments were identified; density per phase

Each phase was walked with the three questions (what she does, what the brand does, what is at stake). Routine moments are mapped per pattern S2-04: payment, the price, allergens, the chair. Density: phase 1 four moments (two online, two on the street or in the mall); phase 2 four (room, face, queue, case); phase 3 eight (the question and its reception, choosing, tasting, the plain facts); phase 4 four (scoop, Note, payment, the box); phase 5 four (chair, card, eating, the mall pause); phase 6 two (the end as two moments); phase 7 four; phase 8 three. Thirty-three in all. The heaviest phase is the question (eight), which is where the differentiator and most of the fact gaps (prices, age line, allergens) sit.

Two counters, one map. Ring Mall is not a second brand expression on this map; it is the same arc with four moments of its own (M04, M07, M24, and M13 to confirm) and two that are tested there (M09, M18). If Evo's answer to open-facts 9 is that the question is not asked at the mall, the mall becomes a second map and this note is reopened.

## 4. Why each HIGH is HIGH

Six of 33 (18 %), all brand-led until the observation move.

- **M06 Someone looks up.** The threshold thin-slice (section 3.2.3, a contested heuristic used as a design rule): the first seconds anchor the visit. The brand's own logic puts the anchor on a person: message 2 ("we say nothing — we look up"), value 12 ("eye contact, names, no scripts"). It runs at both counters, which is why it is HIGH rather than the Rakovski room.
- **M09 The question ★.** Where the differentiator becomes visible (source 3, the brand's logic) and a moment of decision for her (source 2: answer, or not). It is also where the mall counter is tested, and it is HIGH there whatever the queue, because the value "Notice the moment" names the queue as its hard case.
- **M11 The answer received.** Story block 5, the challenge, is here: "the real risk is being met with the generic friendly again". A question asked well and received badly confirms the persona's fear; the acceptance of "nothing" is value 11 in practice and the no-push rule (guardrails BE: no persuasion after a "no").
- **M18 The Note ◉.** The peak by the two-selves logic (evidence tier): the one thing she carries out, the moment the story's transformation (block 6) is made physical.
- **M25 The goodbye, by name ⊐.** The end, HIGH by default (peak-end); and the only moment after the card where a person speaks to her, so the after-state ("seen, lighter") is confirmed or lost here.
- **M26 Attention held ⊐.** The second half of the end. On a counter the leaver and the newcomer share the same face in the same seconds; the goodbye without the held attention is said to the next person. Two HIGH on a thirty-second phase is unusual and deliberate: at a table the end is one moment; at a counter it is a sequence that must be designed as one or it is lost.

## 5. Close calls

- **M05 The door and the room (RK): MEDIUM, not HIGH.** A restaurant map would put the threshold on the room. The brand book says the ritual needs no theatre; Elena's affinities say restraint. The observation test is set: if guests hesitate inside the door before a face turns, M05 moves up.
- **M12–M13 Choosing and tasting: MEDIUM.** Moments of decision, usually HIGH; here the decision is deliberately made light (eight flavours, two tasted, one fact), so cognitive load is low by design (cognitive ease, evidence tier). Message 4 lives here, but the differentiator does not.
- **M17 Scooped without theatre: MEDIUM.** The craft is the mission (message 8), not the differentiator; making it HIGH would make the brand the hero (the Hero/Guide rule).
- **M29 The return: MEDIUM.** "Whether to come back" is a decision moment, but it is made away from the brand; what the brand does at the return is M06 and M12 again, which are already weighed.
- **M07 The queue acknowledged (RM): MEDIUM.** A candidate HIGH if the observation shows the question is dropped or guests leave the queue; held at MEDIUM because M09 carries the mall test.
- **M20 The box of twelve: MEDIUM.** A second persona-situation (the gift buyer) with higher spend; the brand's action is the same question with more time, not a new moment of truth.

## 6. The signature

★ M09. The signature is the brand's identifying ritual made visible, separate from the peak: a guest who has received nothing yet already knows what this place is when she is asked what we are celebrating. It precedes the peak by two or three minutes; the Note (M18) closes what the question opens. They are one ritual for the brand book and two moments for the floor; Stage 3 writes two pages. The recommended editorial tagline is the same line (open-facts 30), which is why it is also the one brand asset judged by the filter test.

## 7. The peak

◉ M18. The remembering self keeps the peak and the end. The Note is the peak because it is unexpected (she asked for gelato and receives a card for her moment), physical (it leaves with her, lands on her desk, gets posted), and hers (drawn by chance, never chosen for her, which is the defaults principle used in the guest's favour: the chance draw removes the suspicion of a sales pick). Two facts are not on file and the page cannot lock without them: who draws it, and whether it is handed before or after payment (open-facts 28). The practice's position for the design: handed before payment, with eye contact, so the till does not end the moment; to be confirmed against the floor, not assumed.

## 8. The end

⊐ M25 + M26, designed as one. The goodbye by name is the words; the held attention is what makes them land. On a counter the end is in competition with the next threshold: the same person is ending one visit and opening another. The design rule passed to Stage 3: the leaver gets the last second before the newcomer gets the first. With one person on the counter at peak this degrades, and the degradation must be named on the page rather than left to improvisation (staffing per daypart: open-facts 47).

## 9. Each silence

- **M08 Reading the case.** Private activity. The choice is hers; a flavour named unasked takes the choice from her and is the category's reflex ("our bestseller"). The brand stands back until she looks up.
- **M10 The beat after the question.** Pattern S2-03, the silence between two HIGH moments: the question needs a second to land as a question and not a slogan. The Bible's own signature phrase carries it: "then a beat of silence". A fourth kind of silence beyond the manuscript's three (between peaks, private activity, transitions): the silence a question requires. Recorded in the harvest.
- **M22 Reading the card.** Private. The card is hers; "which one did you get?" turns the peak into a performance and the chance draw into a game.
- **M23 Eating, talking.** The longest phase and, by duration neglect, the one that adds least to memory; presence here reads as hovering and subtracts from the pause (message 3). No clearing, no "all good?", the sound stays low.

Silent is not LOW: LOW moments (M19, M21, M24) still act a little; the four silences act not at all.
