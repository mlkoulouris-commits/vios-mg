# M28 · HIGH IMPACT · Coming back

In-room · The card on the desk (phase 7) into the next visit's threshold · owner: floor

Dominant inputs: **brand** and **non-verbal**; BE (peak-end's result, status quo); concept (how a small team remembers).

## CURRENT STATE

She decided to return, on the strength of the card and the door. She walks in again. "They remembered my name."

## DO

1. On return she gets M5 with the locked known-guest line if recognised, her name if known; the question is still asked, every visit.
2. Her usual remembered where known (M10's regular line); what is new about her noticed, not announced.
3. How the team remembers: a names habit trained at Stage 4 (face, name, usual), fed by the closing ritual of telling the day's best moment (v3 §4.8, staff micro-story). Nothing written about a guest without consent (guardrails 12). No loyalty card, no stamps, no points: the return is her decision and the brand gives it no mechanism.
4. Letters (M27) only where she gave an address freely; a quiet birthday note only where she gave the date.
5. A regular is never a sales channel: no "bring a friend", no "tell your colleagues".

## SAY

Locked lines belong to M05 and M10: "Good to see you again." · "Pistachio again, or something new today?" Both **pass**.

## DON'T

- "Back again?"; "The usual?" before the question; pretending to remember; recognising her aloud with details ("you were here Tuesday with…").
- Stamps, points, "your fifth visit".

## NON-VERBAL

The smile a notch warmer than for a first-timer, not a performance of recognition (NV C.2); the name at conversational distance (NV B.3).

## BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS

The remembering self acted on last visit's peak and end (BE 7); recognition is the persona's own stated trigger ("If they remember my name, I'll come back"). Status quo (BE 15): the usual is offered as a default she can leave for something new.

## RECOVERY

| If … | → |
| --- | --- |
| She is not recognised | The ritual as for a first-timer, which is complete in itself. No pretending. |
| The wrong usual is named | "Then something new today." No apology stack. |
| She corrects her name | Used correctly from then on, no fuss. |

**State arc:** Returning → Recognised → Asked again

**Success metric:** Staff-counted regulars per week (Bible KPI, raw numbers); on sampled arrivals, regulars greeted by name.

**Target emotion:** known
