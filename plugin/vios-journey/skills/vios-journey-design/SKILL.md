# Vios Journey Design

Stage 3. The experience itself, specified in operational terms so that two staff members trained on it perform it the same way and a trainer who was not in the engagement can teach it. Strategy becomes the room here. Hard lines: `vios-core:vios-guardrails` 5 (food truth in the sensory frame), 9 (claims and allergens in scripts), 10, 11 (escalation topics are recoveries handed to a named person).

## Read first

- `../../references/stage-3-journey-design.md`, the chapter verbatim: seven moves, the design-page template, patterns S3-01 to S3-08, the briefs, the harvest.
- `../../references/section-3-3-three-registers.md`: scripts are written in the floor register, pages in the operational register, reasoning stays in the concept register. Never paraphrase across registers; redraft.
- `vios-core:vios-filter-test`: the brand's filter test. Every script and every guest-facing line passes it before it is locked. No test on file: derive it first.
- `[client]/journey/journey-map.md`, `reasoning-note.md`, `handoff-to-design.md`: the table of contents, the why, and the latitude. A design that contradicts the Reasoning Note goes back to Stage 2.
- `[client]/brand-book-vN.md` (persona, Role, Connection, archetypal mix, expression dimensions, differentiator, messages, story) and `fact-sheet.md` + the operating concept (menu, beverage programme, service model, price point, room, equipment, staffing).
- `vios-core:vios-be-lens` and `vios-core:vios-nonverbal-lens`: the two libraries' five-line versions; cross-reference the full entries by name.

## The four converging inputs

Every page names its dominant inputs: **brand** (who it is for, the Role here, what we want felt), **concept** (what the kitchen, bar, room and staffing allow), **behavioural economics** (which system is deciding, cognitive load, reference point, how the remembering self is formed, where friction is removed or added), **non-verbal** (proxemics, paralanguage, kinesics, haptics, chronemics, environment). Decision moments lean brand + concept + BE; presence moments brand + NV; operational moments concept with the others giving character. No moment is designed with fewer than four consulted.

## Working order (§ 2.3.2.3)

HIGH first, and within HIGH the peak and the end first. Then MEDIUM, then LOW, then the silent moments last (what staff do not do, and where they are while not doing it). The schedule is written down before the first page. **Single-page mode**: when one page is asked for before the set exists (a test, a pilot moment), write it, say on the page that it is out of order, and reference `choreography.md`, `recovery.md` and `schedule.md` as "to be written" with the scenario codes it expects.

## The design page (one per moment)

The manuscript's fields, rendered in the house layout the practice ships (ONE Gallery, 28 pages). Header: M-code · tier label · moment name · descriptor line (type · phase · mode, e.g. "Digital touchpoint · Pre-visit · Self-led", "Environmental · 2–10 min · Approach") · markers. Tier labels: **CRITICAL IMPACT** (HIGH), **HIGH IMPACT** (MEDIUM), **STANDARD** (LOW), **SILENT**. Body blocks, in this order:

| House block | Holds (manuscript fields) |
| --- | --- |
| CURRENT STATE | the moment in one sentence and the customer state: where on the arc, what they just had, what comes next |
| DO | the action as a numbered list: who, from where, places what where; the sensory frame and the room's work appear here as numbered items when the room is doing specific work |
| SAY | the script, marked **LOCKED** where the words are exact; floor register; filter-tested; the caption voice for digital moments |
| DON'T | the failure modes and the tempting defaults, as short prohibitions |
| NON-VERBAL | body, geometry, silence, tone, volume, timing (omitted on digital and silent pages) |
| BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS | the principle at work, cited as "BE n name" (the numbered principles of section 3.1.5, e.g. BE 17 thin-slicing) and what it does to the decision here; non-verbal entries cited as "NV letter.number" (e.g. NV C.2 eye behaviour) |
| RECOVERY | "If … → …": trigger, what is done and said, who takes over; references the recovery document |
| state arc | one line: before → during → after ("Curious → Visualising → Decided") |
| Success metric | an observable signal, never a sentiment |
| Target emotion | one word or two |
| Brand pillar | the core message or pillar the moment serves (welcome, discovery, differentiator, quality) |

The manuscript's eleven questions, for completeness, map onto the blocks as follows:

1. **The moment**, one sentence (from the map).
2. **Customer state**: where on the arc, what they just had, what comes next.
3. **Brand intention** (from band 4).
4. **Action**: who approaches from where, says what, places what where. Precise enough for two people to do it identically.
5. **Script**: only where exact words are required; one or two locked lines; floor register; passes the filter test. "Welcome" is not a script; "Welcome — sit anywhere" is. Where the moment changes with the daypart or the occasion, give the line in **registers** (default · evening · private event) rather than variations.
6. **Non-verbal frame**: body, geometry, silence, tone, volume, timing. Usually larger than the script.
7. **Sensory frame**: light, sound, scent, surface, temperature; or "inherits the room".
8. **BE design**: the decisions the guest makes here and how the design shapes them; empty for low-decision moments.
9. **Role of the room**: sightlines, acoustic separation, flow, lighting register, when the room is doing specific work.
10. **Success metric**: an observable signal ("sat without asking which table"), never a sentiment.
11. **Failure mode** and the recovery reference.

HIGH pages add: the brand-coherence test for this moment (does it hold the Role and the Connection statement), the story-framework block it plays, operational dependencies. MEDIUM pages carry the same blocks, shorter. LOW pages: CURRENT STATE, DO, DON'T, the metric line. SILENT pages: DO is "Nothing" with what M-before did the work, and DON'T lists what would break the silence. Footer: brand-book sections, BE and NV entries, dependencies. Every page is one rendered page; uniform layout across the set.

## Choreography (§ 2.3.2.5)

A separate document, "Choreography & continuities", in three sections, **continuities first**: section 1 the continuities as titled rules (the voice is the same from the caption to the farewell · approach angle and distance · step back after every locked gesture · where the team stands when not at a table · the signature leads, every guest, every daypart · the **moment cluster** around the peak designed as one, every moment serving it · the silences as design); section 2 the transitions as moment pairs (M08 → M09 …) with what the transition does and why it matters, duration and what fills it; section 3 the handoffs (who passes the guest to whom, what is said and shown, what the guest does not see). Name the cluster explicitly (ONE Gallery: M13–M21 protects the peak at M18).

## Recovery (§ 2.3.2.6)

By failure scenario, not by moment: wrong order, wait exceeded, guest visibly unhappy, allergy disclosed late, over-served table, complaint at the bill, plus the scenarios the concept adds. For each: trigger (observable), what staff do, what they say (floor register, filter-tested), who is informed, how the guest is left. Recovery is written in the brand's character (pattern S3-07), not the category default: no over-apology, no under-acknowledgement. Every HIGH moment has a recovery. Escalation topics (guardrails 11) end with a named person taking over, never with a script.

## Patterns to apply by default

S3-08 threshold thin-slice + S3-01 locked greeting + S3-02 silent walk as one composition · S3-03 non-visible information rule · S3-04 one decision anchor at ordering, defended, not drifting · S3-05 peak placement test ("if the evening ended here, would it feel complete?") · S3-06 bookended end that echoes the threshold · S3-07 recovery within character. Record a pattern the engagement used that is not in the chapter, in the standard form.

## Close the design (§ 2.3.2.7)

Six questions, yes/no with the fix: every map moment has a page · every page names its dominant inputs with cross-references · the Role holds at every moment · choreography complete · every HIGH has a recovery · a trainer outside the engagement could train from it.

## Briefs (§ 2.3.6)

- **Operations brief** (10–20 pages), in the house form (ONE Gallery, Oct 2026 revision), eight sections: 1 team structure and the operating principle (named roles, who is not on the floor, position assigns the role in the moment); 2 coverage by daypart as a minimum-staffing table (daypart · hours and mode · minimum on floor · composition), with the rule that a band that cannot be staffed is discussed, never silently degraded; 3 role responsibilities at each HIGH moment (primary actor, coordination, backstop, knowledge requirement); 4 the service-flow assumptions the design makes, each boxed with its reason and the moment it protects (tables per server at the dwell, bar seats per bartender, prep time per signature drink, pass-to-table time, the bandwidth the deliberate pause needs); 5 equipment and layout dependencies, each with the moments it serves and the source (the equipment brief lives here, not as a separate document, unless the build is large); 6 training assumptions (the locked scripts every team member learns, knowledge requirements, drills, the pre-shift briefing); 7 standards and observable metrics as a table (standard · observable behaviour · target frequency), sampled not exhaustive, diagnostic not punitive; 8 management review cadence (daily pre-shift, weekly team review, monthly design review with the founder off the floor) and the rule that small revisions over the first 6–12 months are expected.
- **Equipment and layout brief** as a separate document (6–12 pages) only when the build is large enough to go back to the architect or interior designer; otherwise section 5 of the operations brief.
- Revisions to Stage 1 briefs, versioned and reissued, when the design surfaces a sightline, a graphic element or a material they did not anticipate.

## Output

`[client]/journey/design/` with `pages/M01.md … Mnn.md` in journey order (two-digit codes, the same as the map), `choreography.md`, `recovery.md`, `briefs/operations.md`, `briefs/equipment-layout.md`, `schedule.md` (the design schedule), and `[client]/archive/harvest-stage-3.md` (new patterns, recovery scenarios, library entries, template refinements; half a day, the practice's pattern factory). Claude Docs for the founder's review only when the account manager asks for them, not as part of writing the files. Volume is expected: two to three hundred pages for thirty moments; the uniform template is what keeps it usable.

End with: pages written by tier, HIGH recoveries done, the threshold sequence and the bookended end in one line each, open dependencies, and the next step (`vios-training-programme`).

## Example (one HIGH page compressed; brewery; placeholders)

**M02 · CRITICAL IMPACT · Threshold crossed** · In-room · ~30 sec · Arrival. CURRENT STATE: made the trip, checking it was worth it; the room speaks before anyone does. DO: 1. host at the stand two metres inside the door, turns before the guest reaches it; 2. one beat, then the greeting, then lead; 3. tanks visible from the door, kitchen pass lit, room at 62–66 dB, bread and wort in the air. SAY (LOCKED): "Welcome — sit anywhere." (filter test: not performed ✓). DON'T: no reservation question inside the greeting; no tour on the walk. NON-VERBAL: lens-level eye contact for the line only, open palm toward the room, moderate pace, lead slightly ahead. BEHAVIORAL ECONOMICS: thin-slicing (BE 17), cognitive ease (BE 1); nothing to decide yet. RECOVERY: if the guest hovers → the second welcome (R01). Checking → Settling → In. Success metric: moves into the room without a question within ten seconds. Target emotion: at ease. Brand pillar: welcome.
