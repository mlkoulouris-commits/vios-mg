# Harvest — Stage 3, Atelier del Gelato (2026-11-01)

Per § 2.3.7. Candidate entries from one engagement; promote after a second use.

## New patterns (candidates)

**S3-C1 — The counter end against the next threshold.** When to use: any counter or bar format where the next guest is present as the current one leaves. What it produces: the end as two moments (the line, the held attention) and a two-second rule: shoulders stay with the leaver until past the counter, eyes go up to the newcomer within ten seconds; two bodies at peak, a named degradation with one. What it asks of the brand: staffing the end, not only the welcome. Failure mode: a correct line and a turned back, invisible to the staff and read by both guests.

**S3-C2 — Locked behaviour, no line, at the threshold.** When to use: a brand whose team standard is "no scripts" and whose signature line sits one moment after the door. What it produces: a threshold whose lock is eyes before words within ten seconds, with the locked line (for known guests only) and the signature question following. What it asks: that S3-01 is satisfied by a behaviour, and that the training coaches the look as hard as it would coach a line. Failure mode: staff improvise a greeting formula to fill the absence of one.

**S3-C3 — The blind draw.** When to use: a signature object given by chance. What it produces: the team member draws without looking, does not read it, hands it with a locked line and a fixed gaze sequence (hands, then eyes), then looks away so the guest reads unwatched. What it asks: that the brand gives up choosing the card to fit the guest, which is the temptation every time. Failure mode: a chosen card, which is a message from the brand rather than a surprise for the guest; a watched reading, which is a performance.

**S3-C4 — The question overheard.** When to use: a queue where guests watch the guest ahead. What it produces: the ritual performed at full form and volume for the guest in hand, designed as the waiting guest's social proof; nothing addressed to the queue. Failure mode: shortening under pressure, which teaches the queue the ritual is optional.

**S3-C5 — The no-answer branch.** When to use: a signature question the guest may decline. What it produces: a locked line for "nothing" that keeps the warmth and moves on, the same tasting and the same object regardless; the decline costs nothing. Failure mode: a second try, a joke, a visible drop in warmth; "Small wins count" used as a push.

**S3-C6 — Brief pages for third-party moments.** When to use: moments the brand's Vision places on a partner's table. What it produces: a page in brief form (what the partner does, what it may not do, a periodic check) with a recovery upstream, instead of DO/SAY/DON'T for staff the brand does not employ. Failure mode: scripting a partner's staff.

## Recovery scenario additions

R-ROOM (the room wrong at open; the checklist as the recovery) · R-QUEUE (the named degradation ladder: look holds, tasting shortens, attention becomes eyes only, question and object never) · R-AGE (an alcoholic item in a dessert format; a legal fact, not a brand choice) · R-PARTNER (a partner dropping or altering the brand's object; recovery upstream, quarterly unannounced visit) · a compensation rule (never at the counter by the team member; the approver decides afterwards, privately).

## Library entries

- **BE, friction added before the order** (section 3.1, principle 10): a two-second question before any product word, where the category removes all friction; the effort signals the person over throughput. Atelier M7.
- **BE, endowment through a given object** (principle 12): a card into the guest's hand becomes hers at once and carries the peak home; the brand never asks for it back, nor for a photo of it. Atelier M15, M24.
- **BE, the decision anchor from a fact** (pattern S3-04): the anchor tied to the guest's own answer or to today's batch ("made this morning"), named at pre-shift so it cannot drift to a bestseller. Atelier M11.
- **NV, the gaze sequence at a hand-over** (C.2, D.3): hands, then eyes, then deliberately away. The look-away after a gift is the non-verbal form of the silence after a peak. Atelier M15, M18.
- **NV, eyes to one guest, shoulders to another** (C.4): two guests on one face for two seconds; orientation and eye behaviour split by rule. Atelier M23.
- **NV, the design of a counter wait** (E.2): the ritual for the guest ahead as the content of the wait; nothing addressed to the queue. Atelier M6.

## Template refinements (suggested)

- Name the descriptor types the header expects (In-room · Digital · Environmental · Object · B2B · Operational) and which blocks each drops.
- LOW pages: "SAY only where a line is locked"; four of ten LOW pages here carry one.
- MEDIUM pages: a length rule ("every block, none longer than five items").
- Seed the R-codes on the design schedule before the first page.
- A two-moment end: say whether one page or two.
- New locked lines written at Stage 3 are appended to the filter-test batches and flagged to the approver as new copy.
- Legal facts (an age line) and allergen lines carry a source and a date on the page; the page cannot be locked without them.
- The recovery catalogue: add a counter example and a B2B example to the list of scenarios the concept adds.
- Compensation: a one-line rule in the recovery instruction (who may offer what, and when).
